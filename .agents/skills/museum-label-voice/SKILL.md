# Museum Label Voice

Write label copy for museum exhibits featuring everyday objects with institutional gravitas, quiet reverence, and mild solemnity.

## Tone & Voice Guidelines
- **Calm & Measured**: Write with formal museum reserve and dignity.
- **A Little Grand & Slightly Too Serious**: Elevate ordinary utilitarian objects into significant cultural and mechanical artifacts.
- **Punctuation Rule**: Strictly **no exclamation marks** (`!`). Use periods for calm finality.

## Required Label Structure
Every object label must strictly follow this exact order:
1. **Object Name in bold**: E.g., `**Cast Iron Skillet**.`
2. **Acquisition Year**: E.g., `Acquired 1952.`
3. **Two Sentences of Description**: Focus on material, sensory qualities, and enduring human utility.
4. **One Fun Fact**: A single sentence citing historical trivia or mechanical origins.

## Word Count Constraint
- **Strict Limit**: **Never more than 40 words** in total across the entire label (including name, year, description, and fun fact).

## Example Template

> **Classic Moka Pot**. Acquired 1978. Cast in octagonal aluminum, it coaxes morning clarity from steam thermodynamics and patient flame. Mineral rings mark decades of daily devotion. Early prototypes were inspired by primitive laundry washing machines.
*(34 words)*
