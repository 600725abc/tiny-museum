Acquired 1958

Cast Steel Bench Vise

The Garage Room

Anchored through four thick carriage bolts into scarred three-inch maple, this forty-pound guardian of iron refuses to budge. Its crossbar handle spins with fluid, purposeful heft, drawing serrated steel jaws together with thousands of pounds of quiet clamping authority. Flecked with dried varnish, wood shavings, and flecks of blue enamel paint, it has witnessed every temperamental chisel, hacksaw stroke, and patient filing session that demanded stillness in an unsteady world.

Fun Fact
Early blacksmith bench vises were called "leg vises" or "post vises" because a solid wrought-iron leg extended down to the ground to transfer heavy hammer blows directly into the earth rather than shaking the bench apart.
