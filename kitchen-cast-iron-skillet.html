Acquired: 1952

Cast Iron Skillet

Kitchen Collection • Catalog No. K-02

Forged with unapologetic heft, this ten-inch pan is virtually indestructible. Every sear, blistered crust, and skillet cornbread baked within its walls has contributed microscopic polymer layers of carbonized oil—a living, continuous chronicle of meals shared and remembered. Unlike modern non-stick coatings that deteriorate over time, seasoned iron deepens in intimacy with every passing year, holding heat with patient resilience.

Curator's Note • Fun Fact

A well-maintained cast iron skillet creates a natural non-stick surface through polymerizing fats, often surviving in daily use across four or five generations of a single family.
